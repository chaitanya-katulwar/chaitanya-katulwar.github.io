Overview

When a design system exists but nobody follows it, you don't have a system — you have a suggestion. At BookMyShow, designers were detaching components, creating one-off layouts, and skipping styles entirely. The library had fallen so far out of sync with the product that it was easier to ignore than use.

I rebuilt it solo from scratch as Lexicon — 100+ modular components for mobile and web, paired with usage guidelines that made the right choice the easy choice. It's now the foundation multiple product teams build on every day.

Components

I designed Lexicon’s components to be modular, consistent, and easy to scale across products. Each component has a clearly defined structure and states, making it flexible enough for different use cases while maintaining visual and interaction consistency.

"By ensuring all components map to production variables, we eliminated the design-to-code translation friction entirely."

Guidelines

In addition to the new components, I created Lexicon’s guidelines to establish a shared design language across teams. They define how components should be used and adapted, ensuring consistency, clarity, and quality as the system grows.

The Auditing Process

Before designing a single component, I conducted a thorough audit of the BookMyShow product ecosystem, reviewing over 40 key transactional screens. This audit revealed severe system divergence: we discovered 14 variations of the primary blue button color, 8 different input field styles, and more than 120 detached components across our design files.

To establish a clean baseline, I categorized these elements into critical components, layout patterns, and atomic styling units. By mapping out where the deviations occurred, we could pinpoint exactly why designers were detaching elements—mainly because the old system was too rigid to adapt to complex ticket discovery filters and screen aspect ratios.

Token Architecture

A key milestone in Lexicon's engineering adoption was the introduction of a three-tier design token architecture. Instead of hardcoding hex values directly in CSS or mobile stylesheets, we structured tokens into three distinct layers:

  • Global Tokens: The core foundation of our color, spacing, and font scales (e.g., color-blue-500).
  • Alias Tokens: Semantic definitions that assign meaning to global tokens, allowing them to shift dynamically between Light and Dark mode contexts (e.g., surface-primary maps to neutral-900 in dark theme and neutral-50 in light theme).
  • Component Tokens: The structural bindings unique to specific UI elements (e.g., button-primary-active-bg), shielding components from global variable updates.

Systematic Ecosystem Transformation

Lexicon systematically eliminated compilation discrepancy debt, ensuring multiple product teams ship features 3x faster with absolute visual and interactive consistency across all client touchpoints.

Component detachment rates plummeted from over 65% to less than 2% within three months of deployment, establishing the system as a developer's and designer's favorite material.
