Overview

Every major iOS and Android release is a deadline nobody puts on the roadmap. Miss it, and you risk App Store rejection, jarring visual inconsistencies, and an app that starts to feel out of step with the OS it lives on. At BookMyShow, I owned the design response to platform updates, translating Apple and Google's evolving guidelines into concrete decisions across the product.

The most substantial was Android 15's shift to edge-to-edge displays, which required rethinking how every screen handles the status bar and navigation bar. I designed the guidelines that gave both designers and developers a clear, shared framework for getting it right.

The Risks of Lagging Behind

01

Store Rejection

Missing support timelines on critical OS overrides often results in deployment suspension or immediate App Store rejection by auditors.

02

Jarring Layouts

Uncoordinated safe-area offsets manifest as layout overlapping, clipping text, and rendering broken interfaces on new handsets.

03

Perceived Decay

When application components look and react differently than native OS panels, users perceive the product as neglected or unmaintained.

System Insets

When Google announced that Android 15 would draw content under system bars by default, it meant our standard bottom sheets and floating buttons would clash with native system icons. I led a layout audit to convert static height components into responsive containers that request native system safety paddings.

We mapped the system-drawn heights dynamically, implementing a baseline padding token system. If the bottom system navigation bar is active, the container automatically adds a safety buffer matching the exact system-reported insets, preventing transactional buttons from overlapping the OS pill indicator.

Platform Testing

To verify these guidelines, I established a cross-platform visual check routine. We tested our layouts across a matrix of physical handsets, ranging from the Dynamic Island on the iPhone 15 series to varying punch-hole camera placements on mid-range Android devices.

Each screen layout was verified under dynamic text sizes up to 200% for accessibility support, ensuring that ticket counts and checkout timers remain fully clickable and visible even under severe system overrides.

Continuous Compliance & Quality

I made regular updates to keep the product aligned with the latest iOS and Android platform guidelines. These included visual adjustments, interaction updates, and accessibility improvements driven by evolving policies from Apple and Google.

Ensuring compliance helped maintain a consistent and intuitive experience for users while supporting long-term platform stability across critical product releases.
