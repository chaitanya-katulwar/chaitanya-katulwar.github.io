Airport Hospitality: Coordinating Operational Chaos

Role Lead UX Designer
Scope End-to-End Service System
Platform Desktop & Tablet Interface Ecosystem
Users Supervisors, Dispatchers & Ground Agents

Overview

Managing airport hospitality services means coordinating dozens of moving parts (agents, supervisors, service timings, and traveler expectations), all in real time. At TCS, I led the UX for an internal platform built to bring that complexity under control.

The system spanned two products: a desktop operations portal for supervisors and a tablet app for agents and supervisors with role-based access, covering services from VIP transfers and meet & greet to lounge access and baggage handling. I led the design end-to-end, mapping 20+ service journeys to uncover operational gaps, then translating them into wireframes and final UI that worked for every role, across every touchpoint.

Two Dedicated Platforms

// Desktop Portal

Operations Dashboard

Tailored specifically for airport dispatchers and shift supervisors. Supports multi-flight timeline views, automated agent assignment queues, and high-frequency incident status alerts.

  • Real-time flight arrival data feeds and delay offset trackers.
  • Visual drag-and-drop workforce routing systems.
  • Granular role-based assignment logs for auditing compliance.
// Tablet App

Ground Agent Assistant

A highly mobile utility tool built for terminal gate agents. Prioritizes touch targets, outdoor glare contrast scales, and offline capability for low-network hangar zones.

  • Step-by-step passenger check-in verification scans.
  • Lounge admittance status flags and manual baggage overrides.
  • Immediate supervisor request alerts with coordinate mapping.

Operational Auditing

To design a portal that actually improves efficiency, I spent several days shadowing ground operations agents at Hong Kong International Airport. We mapped out 20+ service journeys, tracing how VIP transfers, meet-and-greets, and baggage handling requests were dispatched. The audit revealed key friction points: supervisors were relying on physical walkie-talkies and paper logs to route agents, causing a 12-minute dispatch delay per passenger transfer.

By mapping out these service journeys, we identified operational gaps where communications broke down. This mapping served as our blueprint, showing exactly how digital updates could replace manual handoffs.

Role-Based UX

We designed distinct experiences tailored to two primary user groups:

  • Operations Supervisors (Desktop): A widescreen console focusing on flight timelines, real-time agent locations, and automated flight delay recalculations. This view allows supervisors to manage terminal-wide resources without searching through multiple tabs.
  • Ground Agents (Tablet): A simplified touch-friendly interface designed for bright gate environments. It features high-contrast tiles, one-click passenger verify scans, and a persistent notification bubble showing active transfer assignments.
