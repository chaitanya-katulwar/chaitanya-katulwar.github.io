The Thesis

Traditional cinema bounds the audience behind a static, linear frame. In my design college graduation thesis at Pearl Academy, I set out to shatter this boundary. Spotlight is an experimental interactive cross-media storytelling platform converting standard cinema capturing reels into controllable spatial user layouts.

Using multi-layered video synchronization, users can scrub, split, and zoom through parallel narrative timelines running concurrently. It translates cinema from a passive consumption medium into an active spatial landscape, letting the viewer direct their own focus in real time.

"By treating screen space as a timeline vector, we allow viewers to step inside the editing process itself."

Interaction Model

The interface divides the browser viewport into responsive geometric canvas panes. As the primary cinematic timeline plays, related b-rolls, metadata overlays, and secondary character tracks load dynamically in the surrounding frames. Users can click any panel to expand it, shifting focus and dynamically re-orchestrating the audio mix based on spatial hierarchy.
